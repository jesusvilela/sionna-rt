## What the notebook tells us — and what it does not

The notebook configures one Munich scene, one transmitter, and **16 receivers** arranged in 8 y-coordinate groups with two receivers per group. It obtains complex 2×2 polarimetric channels by summing path coefficients, discards matrices below norm `1e-12`, then **normalizes each receiver channel separately by its Frobenius norm**. All 16 receivers are reported alive.

Its **five seeds** resample symbols and receiver noise on those **same 16 channel matrices**. They are not five independent propagation environments, and the notebook's stored output contains only **one printed example H**, not a serialized array of 16 channel matrices. Consequently, any new 16-receiver experiment using just this repository would have to rerun ray tracing. This branch intentionally does not.

The printed 8-decimal first-receiver matrix is

\[
H_{\rm printed}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0.92982936+0.33584663i & 0.01006189+0.00676795i\\
-0.06026739-0.00641686i & -0.13674566-0.01022593i
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has Frobenius norm approximately 1 and about **98.105%** of the squared gain in its first transmitter-input column. SNR 20 dB in the notebook gives complex noise variance `N0=0.01`, with `n~CN(0,N0 I_2)`.

## Three fixed, equal-power 16-symbol codebooks

- **Original rank-one**, unchanged from PR #82: `q(x)=Im(conj(x1)x2)=0` for every symbol.
- **Original QPSK²**, unchanged from PR #82.
- **Equal-port circular 16-PSK**, a simple additional off-cone control:

\[
x_k=\frac{e^{2\pi ik/16}}{\sqrt2}(1,i),\qquad k=0,\ldots,15.
\]

All three codebooks contain 16 distinct points with **unit energy per symbol and average power 1/2 on each transmit port**. The new circular control has **exactly 1/2 power in each port in every symbol**, and

\[
q(x_k)=\operatorname{Im}(\overline{x_{k,1}}x_{k,2})=\tfrac12
\]

for all k. It lies entirely **off the original fixed null cone**. In Stokes/Hopf terms its 16 points lie over a *single polarization state*, with distinguishable global phases. This is a physical codebook change, not a compensated frame transformation.

**Resource caveat:** the designs differ in peak port-power pattern, receiver phase sensitivity and polarization-state diversity; matching mean port power does not imply every possible RF resource constraint is matched. No transmitter channel-state adaptation or codebook optimization was used.

## Exact Gaussian bounds, not Monte Carlo confidence intervals

For every pair i≠j with transmitted symbols x_i,x_j, coherent Gaussian ML gives

\[
p_{ij}(H)=Q\!\left(
\frac{\|H(x_i-x_j)\|}{\sqrt{2N_0}}\right).
\]

Let `L(C,H)=M^{-1}sum_i max_{j!=i} p_ij` and `U(C,H)=M^{-1}sum_i sum_{j!=i} p_ij`. The event of preferring one particular competing symbol is contained in ML error, while ML error is contained in the union of all such pairwise events. Thus for **every fixed H**,

\[
\boxed{L(C,H)\le \mathrm{SER}(C,H)\le U(C,H).}
\]

These are conditional *mathematical bounds*, not estimated sampling intervals. Upper bounds are not clipped if they exceed one.

At `N0=0.01`:

| Constellation | Printed Munich H: SER [lower,upper] | Balanced H=I/sqrt(2): SER [lower,upper] |
|---|---:|---:|
| Circular 16-PSK, **off cone** | [0.025844, 0.051824] | [0.025534, 0.051198] |
| Rank-one | [0.068087, 0.106463] | [0.00006490, 0.00012980] |
| QPSK² | [0.165172, 0.414658] | [0.000000287, 0.000001147] |

The intervals are **strictly disjoint** in both columns:

- **Printed Munich H:** circular 16-PSK has lower SER than rank-one, which has lower SER than QPSK².
- **Balanced H:** QPSK² has lower SER than rank-one, which has lower SER than circular 16-PSK.

The strict ordering therefore reverses with channel geometry. In particular, **a non-null-cone, 16-symbol, equal-port-energy codebook provably beats the original rank-one design on the printed channel**, without claiming this holds for the other 15 receiver channels.

On the printed H the average received energies are **0.497347** (circular 16-PSK) and **0.500000** (rank-one and QPSK²). The improvement is not caused by larger average received energy.

The circular codebook is a conventional 16-PSK constellation in the complex line generated by `H(1,i)/sqrt(2)`. A separate one-dimensional Gaussian wedge integral gives **exact conditional model SER 0.05168823** on the printed H and 0.05106856 on the balanced H, both inside the independently computed bounds.

### Does eight-decimal printing invalidate the result?

Assuming each printed real/imaginary entry was rounded to eight decimals, the true channel differs by Frobenius norm at most `sqrt(8)*5e-9=1.4142e-8`. All transmitted vectors have norm one, so a pair distance shifts by at most twice this value. Using the global Gaussian tail derivative bound `|Q'|<=1/sqrt(2π)` at `N0=.01` gives maximum pairwise probability perturbation `7.98e-8`. Consequently, the 16-point lower bound shifts by at most `7.98e-8` and the union upper by at most `15×7.98e-8≈1.20e-6`.

The strict *lower-rank-one minus upper-circular* gap is **0.01626387**, orders of magnitude larger than these uncertainties. The ordering survives any channel values consistent with that rounding hypothesis. The claim is still **conditional on this source output**, not on unknown physical/calibration uncertainty.
